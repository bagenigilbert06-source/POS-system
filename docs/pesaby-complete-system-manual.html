1. What Pesaby is and who it is for

Pesaby is a back-office and point-of-sale system. It joins sales, payments, products, stock, purchasing, customers, expenses, staff, and reports in one workspace.

Business ownersSee sales, profit, cash movement, stock value, expenses, and staff activity.
ManagersControl products, prices, purchasing, inventory counts, transfers, refunds, and daily operations.
CashiersFind products, make sales, accept approved payments, print receipts, and view allowed history.
Stock and purchasing staffOrder from suppliers, receive deliveries, count stock, and move goods between branches.
Setup→Add products→Receive stock→Sell→Review reports

2. First-time setup

Complete the setup checklist before normal trading. The workspace header shows the current business and location.

1
Create the business workspace.

Enter the business name, type, contact information, currency, country, and timezone.

2
Create the main location.

Add its name, code, address, phone, and receipt information. Add other branches later if needed.

3
Choose modules.

Enable the business tools you use, such as POS, inventory, purchases, customers, expenses, and analytics.

4
Configure payments and receipt settings.

Confirm cash, card, M-Pesa, credit, tax, logo, and receipt details.

5
Add staff and products.

Give each employee only the permissions required for their job, then build the product catalogue.

Good practice: finish setup, create a test product, receive a small quantity, make a test sale, and verify the receipt and stock before opening for customers.

3. Staff and access

Role Normal responsibilities
Owner / administrator Full workspace, settings, staff, finance, inventory, and reporting control.
Manager Daily dashboard, products, purchases, inventory approval, refunds, expenses, and reports.
Supervisor Operational review, counts, selected approvals, and branch activity.
Cashier POS sales, customer selection, approved payment methods, and permitted sale history.

Permissions are checked on the server. Hiding a button is not the only protection. Branch access can also restrict a user to one or more locations.

Never share accounts or PINs. Deactivate a staff member immediately when they leave. Review powerful permissions regularly.

4. Products and categories

Create categories first

Categories make products easier to find at the POS and easier to report on. Examples are Beer, Wine, Spirits, Soft Drinks, Food, or Electronics.

Create a product

  1. Enter a clear product name and select its category.
  2. Add an SKU and barcode where available. Active products must not share the same SKU or barcode.
  3. Enter buying price, selling price, unit, pack details, and reorder level.
  4. Choose tracking: none, lot/expiry, or serial number.
  5. Add shelf life and expiry warning days for perishable items.
  6. Add opening stock only when it has been physically checked.

Archive products that are no longer sold. Archiving keeps old sales and reports correct.

5. Point of sale: making a sale

1
Open Point of sale. Confirm the correct branch and cashier.
2
Add items. Search, browse categories, click a product, or scan its barcode.
3
Review the cart. Check quantities, prices, discounts, customer, and totals.
4
Choose payment. Select cash, M-Pesa, card, or an allowed credit method.
5
Complete once. Wait for confirmation, then print or share the receipt.

The system checks available stock at the active branch. A completed sale reduces that branch’s inventory and records cost of goods sold.

If payment is processing, do not click repeatedly. Check the transaction status before starting a second sale.

6. Payments and M-Pesa

CashEnter cash received and confirm the change shown by the system.
M-PesaStart the payment request, confirm the customer phone, and wait for the callback result.
Card or other electronic paymentConfirm the external terminal result before completing the sale.
CreditSelect an approved customer and follow the permitted credit workflow.

M-Pesa reconciliation helps find payments that are pending, failed, unmatched, or completed. A payment confirmation should finalize the related sale only once.

7. Sales history, receipts, and refunds

Sales history shows each transaction, cashier, items, payment, total, and time. Open a sale to review or reprint its receipt.

Refund process

  1. Find the original sale.
  2. Select the returned items and quantities.
  3. Enter a clear reason and choose whether sellable goods return to stock.
  4. Ask an authorized person to approve when required.
  5. Return money through the correct business procedure.

A restocked refund increases stock at the original selling branch and creates a traceable return movement. Damaged returns must not be marked sellable.

8. Inventory control

Stock value Meaning
On hand Physical quantity expected at the branch.
Reserved Set aside for a transfer or unfinished operation.
Unavailable Damaged, quarantined, or otherwise not sellable.
Incoming Ordered or transferred but not received.
Available On hand minus reserved and unavailable.

Stock counts

Select a branch, count physical products, submit the count, and approve the differences. The system records the correction and reason; never change stock directly.

Branch transfers

Create → approve (reserve) → dispatch (leave source) → receive (enter destination). Partial receipt is supported. Cancel before dispatch when the move will not happen.

Lots, expiry, serials, and cost

Expiry goods use the earliest-expiring valid lot first. Serial goods keep one identity per unit. Cost layers preserve the receiving cost, allowing historical margin and stock valuation.

10. Customers, credit, and invoices

Create customer profiles for contact details, purchase history, loyalty, invoices, or credit. Select the customer before completing the sale so the transaction is linked correctly.

  • Review outstanding credit regularly.
  • Record collections against the correct customer and document.
  • Do not extend credit without the business owner’s rules.
  • Keep phone and email details accurate for receipts and follow-up.

11. Expenses

Record business money going out: rent, wages, transport, utilities, repairs, licenses, or other costs. Enter the date, category, amount, description, and supporting reference.

Stock bought for resale belongs in Purchases, not normal Expenses. This keeps inventory value and gross profit correct.

12. Operations

Operations records exceptional activity such as damaged stock, loss, wastage, adjustments, and reviews. Always choose the correct branch and write a useful reason.

The audit trail answers: who acted, what changed, when it changed, where it happened, and which sale, purchase, transfer, or adjustment caused it.

13. Reports and analytics

SalesRevenue, transactions, average sale, product and category performance.
ProfitSales minus recorded cost of goods and operating expenses.
InventoryOn-hand quantity, stock value, low stock, movements, expiry, and adjustments.
Customers and staffBuying behavior, credit, cashier activity, and performance.

Always check the date range, branch, and status filters before using a report. Historical margin uses the cost stored at the time of sale, not today’s buying price.

14. Settings

Settings control the organization, branches, currency, timezone, receipt identity, taxes, payment methods, modules, and access. Test changes that affect checkout or accounting before using them during business hours.

  • Keep the business name and receipt contacts current.
  • Use the correct timezone for daily totals.
  • Review branch access and permissions.
  • Keep M-Pesa credentials private and configured only in secure environment settings.

15. Recommended daily workflow

Opening

  1. Confirm the correct branch, logged-in staff, internet, payment devices, and receipt printer.
  2. Check pending M-Pesa transactions, incoming deliveries, low stock, and urgent expiry warnings.
  3. Count controlled cash according to your business procedure.

During the day

  1. Complete each sale once and issue a receipt.
  2. Receive only goods physically delivered and checked.
  3. Record every refund, expense, loss, payment, and stock move immediately.
  4. Investigate warnings instead of bypassing them.

Closing

  1. Compare cash, M-Pesa, card, credit, and system sales.
  2. Resolve pending or unmatched payments.
  3. Review refunds, discounts, voids, losses, and adjustments.
  4. Check low stock and prepare purchase orders.
  5. Save or review the day’s sales, expense, profit, and inventory reports.

16. Data safety and troubleshooting

If a page does not load

  1. Refresh once and confirm the internet connection.
  2. Check that the user still has permission and branch access.
  3. Try signing out and back in.
  4. Record the page, time, action, and visible error for support.

If stock looks wrong

  1. Confirm the branch and stock state.
  2. Review sales, receipts, transfers, refunds, and adjustments in the movement ledger.
  3. Perform a physical count and submit the difference with a reason.

Important controls

  • Use individual accounts and strong passwords.
  • Back up the database before schema changes.
  • Deploy application code and its database migration together.
  • Never edit production data directly when a normal workflow exists.
  • Restrict financial, refund, purchase, inventory approval, and staff-management permissions.
